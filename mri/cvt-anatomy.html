CVST Anatomy

The venous drainage of the brain, and why thrombosis location determines everything else, from presenting symptoms to the sinuses you check first on MRV.

Dural venous sinuses
Original illustration, K. Cengiz

The superior sagittal sinus runs along the vertex and drains most of the cerebral hemispheres, meeting the straight sinus at the confluence of the sinuses before splitting into the transverse and sigmoid sinuses on each side. The cavernous sinus sits separately, receiving the superior ophthalmic vein, which is why cavernous sinus thrombosis often presents with orbital signs rather than a typical headache.

Cortical veins
Original illustration, K. Cengiz

The superficial middle cerebral vein connects to both anastomotic veins: Trolard superiorly toward the sagittal sinus, and Labbé inferiorly toward the transverse sinus. These anastomoses matter clinically. They're why isolated cortical vein thrombosis can still drain around a blockage and present subtly, unlike a major sinus occlusion.

Prevalence by location
Original illustration, K. Cengiz

The transverse sinus and superior sagittal sinus are the most frequently involved sites, and roughly a third of cases involve more than one sinus at once. This is the main argument for MRV over a single-sequence look: multi-sinus involvement is common enough that confirming one clot shouldn't end the search.
