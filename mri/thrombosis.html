MRI Signal Characteristics
Acute vs Subacute Thrombosis. Quick Reference.
Kayra Cengiz
B.Arch  |  MD Candidate
BU School of Medicine

Signal intensity on MRI evolves as haemoglobin breaks down after clot formation, but the appearance of venous thrombus can vary by thrombus age, sinus, flow, and recanalisation. These patterns can help identify cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVST) on non-contrast MRI. MRI with MRV is a recommended noninvasive study for confirmation; CTV is a reasonable alternative.

Memory aid
Acute = often dark on T2Intracellular deoxyhemoglobin can cause T2 dephasing; SWI/GRE can be particularly helpful, especially for cortical-vein thrombus
Subacute = bright on T1Methemoglobin shortens T1 relaxation; both T1 and T2 bright in late subacute
Chronic = variable signalOrganisation and recanalisation make conventional and susceptibility-sequence appearances variable
