CT Timeline of Ischemic Stroke
Non-Contrast Findings, Normal to Subacute
Kayra Cengiz
B.Arch  |  MD Candidate
BU School of Medicine

Five checkpoints on the same non-contrast CT: what a normal scan looks like, and how a left MCA territory infarct evolves through it over time. There is also a well documented window later in this timeline where the infarct can transiently blend back toward normal density, hiding a real deficit.

Memory aid
Normal = your comparison pointEvery later stage is judged against this baseline; always compare with the contralateral side
Early = often looks normalCytotoxic edema starts immediately but takes hours to become visible; subtle insular ribbon loss and lentiform nucleus blurring are the earliest clues, and CT here mainly rules out hemorrhage before thrombolysis
Established = the early clues become definiteThe insular ribbon and lentiform nucleus, only subtly blurred earlier, are now clearly obscured; hypodensity is well defined and follows the vascular territory
Late = mass effect building toward its peakSulcal effacement and ventricular compression increase as edema builds toward days 3 to 5; hemorrhagic transformation remains an important complication, particularly after reperfusion
Subacute = the fogging trapAround 2 to 3 weeks the infarct can transiently return to near-normal density as macrophages move in; do not let this reassure you. True encephalomalacia and ex-vacuo dilation are later, chronic findings, typically not seen until well beyond 3 weeks

Note: Beyond this timeline, over 3 weeks out, the infarct progresses to encephalomalacia, a well-defined CSF-density area of tissue loss with ex-vacuo dilation of the adjacent ventricle. The fogging effect can also occur on MRI (T2/FLAIR), typically slightly later, median around 10 days post-stroke. Always correlate with clinical history and prior imaging when a follow-up scan looks unexpectedly normal.
