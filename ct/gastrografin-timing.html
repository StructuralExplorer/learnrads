Gastrografin Transit Timing
Where the Contrast Is, at Each Checkpoint
Kayra Cengiz
B.Arch  |  MD Candidate
BU School of Medicine

Water-soluble oral contrast (gastrografin) has two separate jobs: opacifying bowel for a diagnostic CT, and acting as a therapeutic and prognostic test in adhesive small bowel obstruction. Both depend entirely on timing. Tap through the checkpoints below to see where the contrast has actually gotten to, and what that tells you at each stage.

Note: Exact transit timing varies with the underlying pathology and degree of obstruction, so these windows are typical ranges, not fixed rules. Hyperosmolar water-soluble contrast should be used cautiously when aspiration is a concern. Always correlate with the clinical picture and institutional protocol.
